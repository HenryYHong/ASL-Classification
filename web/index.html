ASL fingerspelling recognizer

MediaPipe Hands finds the hand, a random forest reads the handshape, and a state machine decides when a letter was actually signed. All of it runs in this tab.

The model was trained on one signer across three sessions. Measured leave-one-session-out accuracy is 0.680 for the static letters and 0.889 for J and Z over 118 independent gestures; your hands are not that signer's hands, so expect worse than those numbers here.

Fingerspelling only: 26 letters, one at a time. No words, no grammar.

Camera

The camera feed is read by this page and nothing else. Frames go to a MediaPipe model running in WebAssembly in this tab, are turned into 21 landmark coordinates, and are discarded on the next frame. There is no server to send them to: this page is static files, the recognizer is JavaScript, and no image, landmark or letter ever leaves your browser. Stopping the camera or closing the tab ends it.

v_bar is palm-widths per second, smoothed over -- s; sigma is how far the handshape has drifted from its own trailing median, in palm units. A letter is emitted when a hold is first established, so holding A for ten seconds gives one A. J and Z are only attempted after the hand is parked in their launch pose and then moves — if the J gate fraction never reaches the arming line, J is unreachable no matter how well you sign it.

Problem

Thresholds are the archive defaults: percentiles measured on one camera at about 15 fps, not on yours. They are what decides that a hand is still, or that a gesture has begun.

The model file is about 8 MB and MediaPipe's WASM bundle is another 9 MB; both are fetched once and cached by the browser. J and Z need the motion branch, which fires only on a deliberate, complete gesture and abstains rather than guess.

G, M, S and T are the letters that drift most between sittings and are the likeliest to be read as H, E, E and N. That is a data limitation, not a threshold to tune.
