How it works

Fingerspelling only — one letter at a time, no ASL word signs, no grammar. MediaPipe finds 21 hand landmarks; a random forest reads the handshape; a state machine decides when a letter was actually signed. J and Z are motion letters, so they need a launch pose held still, then the stroke. Drop your hand for a second to end a word: if one real word clearly fits the letters (common words first, from a frequency list), the page names it underneath, and if several fit or none do, it says nothing. Video stays on your device.

Trained on one signer across four sessions plus 8,561 frames of other people's hands from three public landmark sets. If you are not the author, the numbers to plan around are the ones measured by holding out other people. Holding out one signer at a time across ten named signers: 0.9406 across three seeds (0.9379–0.9453), over all 23,984 of their held-out frames. Holding out a whole second set the forest never saw — 1,874 frames from multiple participants captured with this page's own landmarker — 0.8292 across three seeds (0.8212–0.8362). A third set, five signers nothing here is ever trained on, reads 0.889. Run through this page's own logic rather than frame by frame, 266 video clips of other people fingerspelling gave the right letter on 96, the wrong letter on none, and silence on the other 170.

The author's own folds are higher and they are not yours: 0.926 of 4,878 held-out frames with each of his sessions held out in turn (95% interval 0.877–0.965 over the 57 recorded holds), and 0.895 on the one fold recorded on a different day (0.8848–0.8949 over three seeds). The previous release measured 0.910 and 0.870 on the author's folds and had no by-signer number at all. J and Z: 0.95 over 102 events (74 J/Z gestures and 28 movements) in 80 prompted items, cross-validated by prompted item (the labels changed in an earlier release, so that replaces rather than improves on the earlier 0.864). Accuracy varies with hand shape and lighting; across days the author's M is read on about one frame in eight (a data problem with the 2024 recording), and on other people's hands U and R are read as each other, with C, S and G next least reliable.

Numbers mode (experimental) reads the ASL digits 0–9 with a separate forest trained on 218 signers from a public dataset (Sign Language Digits Dataset, Ankara Ayranci Anadolu High School, Apache-2.0), not on the author. It has not been verified on the author's hand or on live video: 0.986 leave-signer-out on that dataset's photos, and 96% of the author's O, V, W, F and B frames read as 0, 2, 6, 9 and 4. J and Z are off in this mode, and a relaxed hand can read as 0 or 1. On the author's logged idle holds, a relaxed hand reads as a digit (mostly 0 or 1) about one time in six in this mode. No word is suggested in this mode.
